Lecture 4 · Many dimensions
Data can have more dimensions than space
Aggregation: too many items? Show summaries such as counts, medians and ranges.
Also: time series (one dimension is time) · semantic zooming
Lecture 4 · Linked displays
Split the dimensions, then link them
Brushing: select items in one view and they light up in every other view.
Attribute Explorer · scatterplot matrix · scagnostics · projections (SOM, t-SNE)
Lecture 4 · Parallel coordinates
Every dimension gets its own axis
Axis = one dimension
Line = one item, crossing every axis
Brush an axis range to filter
Lecture example: 16 manufacturing attributes. Filter for top yield and quality, then few defects, and find the best compromise.
Lecture 4 · More tools
Other views for other questions
Mosaic displays
Tile areas show how categories depend on each other.
Simpson's paradox
A trend in the total can reverse inside every group. Comet charts expose it.
Star plots
Each item is a star; each spoke is a dimension. Compare shapes.
Query by relevance
No exact match? Rank items by distance from the query, closest in the center.
Also: star plots + PCP · dust & magnet · Chernoff faces

Lecture 5 · Color and size
Color is powerful and easy to misuse
Distinguish only 5–10 categories
Vary brightness, not just hue
Never rely on color alone
Bright colors for small areas
Size: selective magnification redraws a map by electoral votes while keeping neighbors adjacent.
Also: HSL color model · two-tone coloring · ColorBrewer palettes · sound
Lecture 6 · Relations
A graph is nodes connected by links
Phone calls, subways, social networks: for graphs, connectivity matters more than any single property.
Lecture 6 · Graph drawing
Node placement decides what people see
Layout goals
Fewer crossings · even link lengths · fewer bends
Where do nodes go?
Known positions (a subway map): draw the links well.
Free positions: group well-connected nodes, or use a circle.
Big graphs
Edge bundling: route links together like cables.
Level of detail: more appears as you zoom.
Left: my HW4 layout, 10 nodes · 15 straight links · 1 crossing (Figma)

Lecture 6 · Sets
Showing what groups have in common
Parallel Sets
Ribbons show how items flow across a few categories, like Sankey diagrams.
Venn diagrams break down after a few sets. UpSet lists every overlap as a bar you can sort.
