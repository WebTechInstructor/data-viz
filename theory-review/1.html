Lecture 1 · What it is
Visualization amplifies thinking
To visualize: to form a mental image of something.
01
External memory
The picture holds the problem so your head doesn't have to.
02
Insight, not decoration
For discovery, decisions, learning and presentation.
03
Not only on computers
Paper works. Interaction usually makes it better.
Information visualization: abstract data with no physical shape (text, statistics, finance, networks). Scientific visualization: data with real geometry.

Lecture 2 · Perception
Some things we see before we look
Pre-attentive processing
Seen in under 250 ms, in parallel, without focused attention.
Use it to find a target, see boundaries and estimate counts. Combining features can break it.
Gestalt: we group what is close, similar, closed, continuous or symmetric.
Lecture 2 · Honesty
Charts can mislead at a glance
Visualization mirage
The first-glance message isn't supported by a closer look at the data.
McNutt, Kindlmann & Correll, CHI 2020
Also watch for: pictures scaled in two dimensions (Tufte), transparency, sort order, resampling.

Lecture 2 · Excellence
Tufte: tell the truth with the least ink
Maximize data-ink
Most of the ink should show data.
Avoid chartjunk
Decoration that distracts and informs nothing.
Multifunction elements
One element both shows data and structures the design.
Small multiples
Repeat one simple chart side by side.
Show causality
Reveal mechanism, process and change.
To critique any chart
What does it show? Could it be simpler? Was it tested?
Most ideas, least time, least ink, smallest space, and always the truth.
Tufte, 1983 · Critique questions after Marti Hearst (Lecture 2)
